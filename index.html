Efficient, under-scaled, and measured on one number

A blended CPL of £5.98 is well inside the £10 benchmark. That is good news and a warning: budget is not what holds the account back, it is losing on ad rank, and it cannot yet see which leads become customers.

Efficiency held while spend nearly doubled

Weekly spend rose from £2.8k to £5.3k between 1 June and 3 August. CPL stayed between £5.30 and £6.14 in every week up to 10 August. That is a stable engine, not a lucky week.

Conversion rate is strong where there is enough data

Taking only search terms with 100 or more clicks, everything outside Budget converts at about 30%. Various 6 tCPA is at 40%, Growth at 36% and Motorly Brand at 26%. Budget is the exception at 6%.

Rates are as reported by Google Ads and still need checking against the CRM.

Specialist credit intent has real depth

IVA, guarantor and bad credit searches cost about £3 to £3.60 a click yet return a CPL of £6.1 to £6.9. Key worker and blue light terms land at £5.23. There is demand here, which is also why it needs watching (see concerns).

Targeting is disciplined

Exact match accounts for 80% of spend in the top search terms and broad match for 8%. Someone has been controlling query quality rather than leaving keywords loose.

Rank, not budget, is the limit

The account spends about £3.4k a week against £5,300 of weekly budget, or 65% (96% in the three healthy weeks before the incident). Three campaigns spend above their weekly budget and the rest use between 6% and 75% of theirs. Lost impression share to budget is 0% everywhere, while nine of ten campaigns lose 77% to 95% of eligible impressions to rank. Quality Score, ad relevance, expected CTR and landing page experience are where we are losing.

Status warning: five campaigns show "Eligible (Limited): search volume limited" and two carry policy flags. The usual causes are low-ranking or non-serving ads, or disapprovals. I want to know exactly which applies to each.

One bidding model and one channel

All ten campaigns use Target CPA and everything is Search. If one signal fails, everything fails with it, which is what happened in August. There is no second goal, no fallback strategy and no remarketing.

Budget converts worst

Its terms with 100 or more clicks convert at 5.9% against about 30% elsewhere, and its CPL of £10.31 is the only one over benchmark. Payment-led searches such as "cars for £150 a month" probably attract browsers rather than applicants.

Brand takes attention it has not earned

Motorly Brand is only 6% of account spend, but "motorly" is the single biggest search term at £1,796 (21% of top-term spend once brand variants are added). Brand is warm traffic that may have arrived anyway, so extra spend here does not buy new customers. Growth budget belongs on colder traffic and competitors. I would look before cutting (see the plan).

Positioning and compliance

A third of top-term spend (33%) is IVA, bad credit and guarantor searches. Motorly risks becoming known as the lenient option. That affects which partners want the leads, approval rates, and compliance on wording such as "guaranteed approval".

CPL alone hides quality

Bike & Caravan gives 15% of conversions from 6.6% of spend at £2.62. Without it, blended CPL is £6.57. Brand also leaks into non-brand campaigns: £424 and 79 conversions in the search terms supplied. Nothing shows whether cheap leads are contacted, approved or funded.

Find where rank is being lost

Audit Quality Score components by ad group: expected CTR, ad relevance and landing page experience. Fixing the weakest component on the biggest campaigns buys volume that exists today, with no extra budget needed.

Bid on value, not on an action

Work out what a good conversion is: product value, preferred contact method, choices made on the form. Turning that into a conversion value goal is more sustainable than a target per action, and it stops one action from starving the whole account.

Shift growth from brand to colder traffic

Hold brand steady, then aim new money at generic, specialist and competitor searches where customers are harder to win. That is where extra volume actually comes from.

Harvest the terms we already pay for

186 of the top search terms are not yet keywords, and 88 of them have 5 or more conversions at £8 CPL or better: £6.1k of spend and about 1,200 conversions. Exact match would give control over bids, copy and landing page.

Test devices and landing pages

Desktop users tend to finish longer forms, mobile users drop off sooner. A short desktop-weighted test, judged on lead quality by device, is a quick win and shows where the valuable customers are.

Changes I would make early

  • Fix tags and errors found in the audit, then upload the lost conversions and check them against Google's own reporting.
  • Brand exclusions and shared negatives for dealer names, US phrasing and non-car vehicles. Look at what the broad match terms are actually matching.
  • Quality Score work on the biggest campaigns: ad copy relevance, landing page match, assets.
  • Clear the status warnings: disapproved and limited ads, and what triggers "search volume limited".
  • A short device test weighted to desktop, reading lead quality by device.
  • Cut wasted budget through negatives, once tags are verified, to fund the next stage.
  • Alerting on spend, impressions and conversion rate so the next break is caught in hours.

What I would deliberately not change

  • No campaigns or ads shut down yet. Failing tags may mean conversions are not being counted fairly.
  • Brand is not cut yet. First learn how those customers behave and what the broad match terms are.
  • No reactive target or budget changes. Treat the Maximise Clicks numbers as unproven, and step back to the original plan slowly.
  • No Display or AI campaign types until the base problems are fixed and the CRM is linked. Fix the boat before we sail.
  • No value bidding until the value data is validated.
  • Nothing judged on data from 24 August onward.

What I would request, and why

The workbook is a snapshot. These are the extra items I would ask for in week one, and what each one settles.

Error audit

Google Ads change history, site error logs and Google Tag Manager errors. A tracking or site fault is the cheapest explanation for the step down, and change history covers both campaign and account.

Timestamped CRM data matched to click and page

Each lead with its click ID, landing page link or template and campaign. It shows the path people took and how behaviour differs by campaign and page.

The sales team's view of quality

Do people who fill in the form convert better than people who phone? Do many start the form and give up? Where are the valuable customers? This defines what a "good" conversion is.

Device split, with CRM outcome

Desktop and mobile behave differently. Knowing outcome by device shows whether to weight spend to desktop, and what mobile drop-off costs.

Value of each product

Revenue or commission per funded customer, by product and partner. Needed to build a conversion value goal.

Partner rules, caps and rejection reasons

Shows whether bad credit and guarantor leads are wanted, and whether the account is drifting into a lenient reputation.

Quality Score, ad strength and policy status

By keyword and ad group. This pinpoints where rank is lost and what triggers "search volume limited" and disapproval flags.

Last year's same weeks, plus Auction Insights

Tests whether late August is seasonal, and whether a competitor changed the auction.

Scale where the marginal economics work

The goal is more volume at a cost per funded customer the partners can support, not the lowest average CPL. Average CPL hides what the next pound actually buys, so growth has to be stepped and checked.

Recover the baseline

Restore the £5k a week at about £5.70 that the account ran at before 17 August. Nothing else is worth measuring until this is stable.

Gate: two clean weeks with CRM leads and Google conversions within 10% of each other.

Cut waste, take the quick wins

Negatives, brand exclusions, status fixes and a desktop-weighted device test. Money saved here funds the next stages.

Gate: tags verified first, so nothing is cut on false data.

Buy the rank we are missing

Improve Quality Score components and ad rank: ad relevance, landing pages, assets, exact-match structure. Then step Target CPA up in a 50/50 experiment on the largest campaign.

Gate: incremental CPL under the allowable CPL for two weeks, in steps of 15% or less.

Raise conversion rate and lead quality

One landing page per intent, faster forms, honest pre-qualification. A better funnel makes every other lever cheaper.

Gate: A/B test with a pre-agreed sample size, judged on funded rate as well as form fills.

Add a second surface, carefully

A low-budget remarketing campaign first, then Demand Gen as the natural step up from Search. Import to Microsoft Advertising once wasted budget is cut: an older audience at a cheaper click. AI Max for Search is worth a capped experiment with brand exclusions and a hold-out, so we can read the incremental effect. Display and the other AI campaign types wait until the CRM is linked.

Gate: base problems fixed and CRM data flowing, so new spend is judged on the same yardstick.

Rules for scaling

  • Step, do not jump. No more than 15% extra spend a week, so bidding has time to learn.
  • Judge marginal, not average. Blended CPL can look fine while each new lead costs far more.
  • Hold when the marginal CPL passes the limit, or when funded rate falls.
  • Two clean weeks before the next step.
  • Never scale on an unverified tag.

Bring Contacted, Approved and Funded into Google Ads

Lead volume tells Google which clicks produce forms. Contacted, Approved and Funded tell it which clicks produce customers. The CRM is not the only data point, but it is the one that changes decisions most.

Capture the click ID

Store gclid, gbraid and wbraid with the lead at submission, with timestamp and consent state.

Add a fallback key

Enhanced conversions for leads: hashed email and phone, so leads without a click ID can still match.

Timestamp every stage

One lead ID, with Contacted, Approved and Funded stamped in the CRM, plus rejection reasons.

Automate the upload

Scheduled API upload with click ID, action, event time, value and a unique order ID for de-duplication.

Promote and optimise

Once trusted, the deepest stage with enough volume, or a conversion value, becomes the bidding signal.

How it informs optimisation and bidding

Three phases, each with an exit test, so bidding only follows data the business already believes.

Observe

Stages imported as secondary goals. Reports show cost per contacted, approved and funded by campaign, keyword and search term. Budgets and negatives start to move on this evidence. Bidding does not.

Optimise to the deepest usable stage

Where a campaign has 30 to 50 conversions a month at a stage, bid to it, usually Contacted or Approved. Small campaigns share data through a portfolio strategy. Funded is too slow and sparse to bid on directly.

Bid on conversion value

Give each stage an expected value from the product, contact method and funded rate. Bid to that value instead of a fixed target per action, so no single action can starve delivery again.

Lag is the trap. Funding can take weeks, and the account's known lead delay is 1 to 2 days. I would report by click date, compare against by-conversion-date views, and use stage-to-funded rates from mature cohorts to value recent leads instead of waiting.

How I would confirm it

Change history first, because it covers both the campaign and the account. Then the site and the tags, and only then the outside world.

  • Change history: any edit to targets, goals, budgets or settings, and any auto-applied recommendation. The odd target decimals (£9.38, £7.14, £8.12) suggest recent edits.
  • Website: was the site up, were the forms working, was there any error on the days around 17 August?
  • Tags: are they still firing, and on which pages? Check Google Tag Manager and conversion action status.

  • Outside factors: last year's same weeks, Google Trends, and Auction Insights.

What the Maximise Clicks response tells us

I read the test as Various 6 tCPA, the only campaign whose spend rebounded at a CPC of £2.90 against a £3 cap. Change history should confirm it.

Baseline: 27 Jul to 10 Aug. Test: week of 31 Aug. Internal figures use the brief (about £400 and 15 leads) against Google's click count.

  • It is a temporary fix, not a strategy. Delivery returned, so bids were what was blocking it. That part is useful to know.
  • It buys volume, not quality. About £27 a lead against about £6 before. Maximise Clicks has no idea which clicks convert.
  • The stats could be false. Google saw 10.5 leads where the CRM saw 15. Until the data is looked at properly, neither number is proven.
  • So use it to learn, then leave it. The aim is a stable position, not a permanently cheap click.

What I would do next

Proactive, not reactive. Quick and careful, in this order.

  1. Review the temporary fixes.End Maximise Clicks and any other stopgap. Go back to the original plan slowly, campaign by campaign, brand first. Not all at once.
  2. Review the data around the crash.Change history, site, tags. Upload the lost conversions from the last two weeks and check them against Google's reporting.
  3. Fix the goal set-up.Use primary and secondary goals, so one action failing cannot starve the whole account again.
  4. Set up early warning.Alerts on tracking, delivery and conversion rate against a 14-day baseline, so a break is caught in hours.
  5. Plan the conversion value goalalongside the CRM link, so bidding follows value once the data is trusted.

The bidding strategy, in three stages

Each stage is only entered when the one before is stable.

Now: Maximise Conversions, capped

Collect clean data with a daily budget near 40% to 50% of the old run rate. Apply a data exclusion for the affected dates. Restart brand first, then Various 6 tCPA.

Next: Target CPA, loose then tighter

Return to Target CPA at 1.5x to 2x the last verified CPA (about £10 to £12), not the old £8. Once a campaign has 30 conversions in 30 days, lower the target by 10% to 15% a week. Test it 50/50 against Maximise Conversions.

Later: a conversion value target

When CRM stages and values are validated, bid to value (Maximise Conversion Value or Target ROAS). Target CPA stays as a fallback for small campaigns, pooled in a portfolio.

Any diagnostic test on a live campaign gets a hard budget of £50 a day and a named end date.
